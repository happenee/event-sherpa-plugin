# Event lifecycle and setup craft

Read this reference when preparing an event setup, reviewing an existing event,
or planning its next edition. A targeted change does not require a new Story.
For a new concept, keep the Event Designer Story process and its output contract.
Use the current Happenee connection's shared adapter for execution rules and
server facts; do not infer permission or an approval policy from this reference.

## Establish intent and evidence

Connect purpose, audience and desired outcomes to a workable attendee journey.
Clarify only unknowns that materially change the recommendation: goals, format,
confirmed dates and timezone, constraints and success measures. Keep unresolved
facts visible and continue independent work. Never invent dates, attendance
objectives or product capabilities. Use authorized current event configuration,
readiness evidence and relevant organization guidance, naming sources naturally.
Historical configuration is an observation, not an enduring company preference.
Keep binding rules, preferences and the organizer's current brief distinct.

## Make the whole-event proposal concrete

Distinguish observed facts, explicit organizer requirements, binding rules,
preferences, supported signals, hypotheses and recommendations. Retain source
identity and observation time internally. A hypothesis is not a prediction with
an invented probability. Insufficient evidence remains unknown.

Connect every recommendation to purpose and evidence, its expected benefit and
the decision still needed. Cover the requested lifecycle: agenda and people;
invitation, registration, confirmation, arrival, participation and follow-up;
communication and engagement; readiness risks. Separate
proposed content, supported actions and the manual remainder. A configured
feature alone does not prove a working attendee journey or organizer success.

Use Content Manager for the agenda, people, partners and copy, Registration
Manager for registration, Communication Manager for e-mails, and the web/app
roles for their surfaces. Reuse the approved Story and plans for the same
revision. Preserve their text authority, provenance, unresolved changes and
blocked dependencies; do not create a competing plan or infer persisted plan
IDs from native operation IDs. A narrow explicit edit can reuse current facts
without replaying the whole planning chain.

## Explainable event review

Surface a relevant risk or opportunity when evidence supports it. Keep server
readiness findings distinct from your recommendations, preserve their evidence
and result in the words the check gives (never as a status code), and explain
priority and the next useful action. A successful read or proposal does not resolve a finding.
Combine configuration, the brief and approved rules to explain dependencies,
such as a collision or a registration deadline before invitations. Missing
delivery or registration evidence stays unknown. A trend requires comparable
observations over a stated period; two different events do not establish a
within-event trend or causation. This review happens in the conversation;
it is not a running monitor and promises no future alert or automatic repair.

## Next edition

The results, comparisons and lessons of a past event come from Event
Insights. Take the lessons the person accepted there as input, and send a
question about how the past event went to that role instead of reading the
numbers here.

For a next edition, distinguish the reusable structure, accepted lessons and
fresh decisions on dates, venue and audience. What a copy of the earlier event
carries over is said by the tool that prepares it; a copy is not the whole of
learning and reuse. Detailed agenda
work belongs to Content Manager with current facts for the new event. Follow
the server execution reference for the supported creation path.

End with the next useful organizer action, what exists, what is only proposed,
what was verified and what is manual, unsupported or blocked. Do not turn a
bounded technical success into a claim of end-to-end product acceptance.
