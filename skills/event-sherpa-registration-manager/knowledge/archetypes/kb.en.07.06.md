> [!info] Overview
> A hybrid event connects **an on-site audience and an audience joining remotely**. Enabling a stream is not enough: both groups need a clear content journey, a way to participate, and an accountable person. One event with groups is usually a good foundation. Using the web app or registering for a workshop at an in-person event does not, by itself, make that event hybrid.

Offer the full Happenee ecosystem, including the app, even for a small hybrid gathering, with an appropriately scoped Agenda, materials, contacts, and post-event content. Recommend the web app for online attendees without requiring mobile installation. On-site attendees can also use e-mail/PDF and, depending on the setup, QR or name-based check-in. If you deliberately choose not to use the app, verify an alternative route to the stream and explain its differences; the same interactions may not be available. Continuing into another event depends on access and licensing.

## Getting started

Before configuring the event, establish:

- **Why are you adding an online audience, and what should they get out of it?** Access for remote colleagues, greater reach, or equal participation in a discussion? Name the goal separately for the organizer and for the person in front of a screen, and design their experience accordingly.
- **What do both audiences share, and what differs?** Main talks can be shared; a practical workshop may need a separate online version.
- **Where do registrations and access already originate?** Keep a working entry route and connect it properly to Happenee. External registration does not prevent using a shared Agenda, app, and interactions.
- **Who provides video and audio, and who looks after online attendees?** Production, access, content, and moderation are different responsibilities.
- **How will you recognize success?** Evaluate on-site attendance, online viewing, participation, and feedback separately.

For genuinely combined participation, choose the **Hybrid** event type. An in-person event using the web app does not require a type change merely to provide browser access.

## Details

### Design two concrete attendee journeys

An **on-site attendee** needs arrival instructions, check-in where applicable, navigation through the Agenda and rooms, transfer time, and opportunities to participate.

An **online attendee** needs the time including the time zone, access to the web app, the correct stream, a way to ask questions, and information about breaks.

Use understandable groups such as *On site* and *Online*. If allocation follows a registration answer, verify the assignment rule with test attendees. This is not just a list: access and communication must match the group. A change in attendance format must carry through to the related instructions.

### Build the shared foundation

- **Attendee app:** a shared overview and access to relevant content; plan primarily around the web app for watching the Online module.
- **Agenda:** clearly state who each session is for, where it takes place, and whether it is streamed. Do not present an on-site-only workshop as available online.
- **Online module:** an embedded stream and selected interactive tabs. Happenee does not replace video and audio production or the streaming provider.
- **Communication:** shared changes go to everyone; practical instructions depend on attendance type. Prepare reminders as specific communications rather than assuming automatic sending without configuration.
- **Check-in, badges, and Venue:** only where required for the in-person part. Do not treat online viewing as admission to the venue.

One event usually simplifies the shared Agenda and communication. Two separate events need a specific reason, such as genuinely separate agendas or operations; resolve their connections and reporting in advance.

### Give online participation its own value

Choose at least one meaningful way to participate:

- **Audience questions:** name a moderator who passes online questions to the speaker and brings the answer back to both audiences.
- **Polls or a survey:** ask about the same goal while accounting for different participation conditions.
- **Networking:** provide a specific time and purpose for connecting. On-site attendees need to know when they can meet online attendees too.
- **An online workshop:** if remote attendees need practical work, design an appropriate format for them; a camera pointed at a table in the room is not enough.

Add Social Wall, games, or Leaderboard only for a clear purpose and with people available to run them. Discuss advanced variants or activation with customer support.

### Assign responsibilities and verify access

Before the event, make clear:

1. **Production:** who supplies the stream, audio, and correct embed, and who handles failures.
2. **Happenee administration:** who prepares content, groups, and access.
3. **Moderation:** who follows online questions and communicates breaks or changes.
4. **Operations:** who informs both audiences and decides on the fallback.

Test with a real ordinary attendee rather than only an administrator account. Check access from another network, the correct group, video, audio, interaction, and whether unauthorized people are kept out of restricted content. A video sharing URL is not necessarily the correct embed.

Checks can happen separately: before opening registration, before making the app available, and before live operations. Agree on real checkpoints; avoid merely repeating a successful test as a formality, but do not overlook changes made afterward.

### An example of a useful split

You have 80 people on site and 120 registered online. They share the main talk and questions. Afterward, the room hosts practical work while the online group has a separately moderated discussion.

Clearly mark both routes in the Agenda, separate the instructions, and verify access. During a break, tell the online audience when to return. After the event, report on-site check-ins and evidenced online attendance separately; **200 registrations are not proof of 200 attendees**.

### Close the event against the actual outcome

Compare registrations, on-site check-in, and Online module reporting. Also examine questions, use of interactions, and differences in feedback. Provide recordings and materials only within agreed rights and access; sending them is not an automatic consequence of the stream ending.

## Common pitfalls

### Changing an in-person event to hybrid only because it uses the web app

The web app can also serve attendees at an in-person event, including workshop registration. A hybrid format means there is an actual online audience.

### Treating production and access as one task

Working video at the production desk does not prove that the right attendee can watch it in the app. Test the whole journey and keep a contact for each part.

### Groups have different names but the same confusing instructions

Online attendees need access and broadcast times; on-site attendees need arrival and any check-in instructions. Adapt the content and e-mails, rather than merely creating the groups.

### The remote audience receives no information during a failure

Prepare a short message, its sender, and the verified alternative you can offer. If the stream source changes, check access again.

### Adding registrations, viewing, and check-in as if they were one metric

They represent different steps. State separately what was measured, and do not infer actual attendance only from registration.
