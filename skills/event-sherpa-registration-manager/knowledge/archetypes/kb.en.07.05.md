> [!info] Overview
> For an incentive trip, retreat, or multi-day itinerary, Happenee helps keep **the Agenda, practical information, and changes together**. Start with who is actually attending, what they still need to confirm, and who needs which information. The attendee app can also benefit a small or recurring group. The right scope depends on changes, locations, choices, and responsibilities—not a fixed attendee count.

We recommend offering the app for every trip, including one for 30 people, as part of the full Happenee ecosystem. It can contain just the necessary itinerary, materials, contacts, and any tickets—and help people return to content or join the next trip, subject to access and licensing. Do not require installation: attendees can choose the web or mobile app, or receive e-mail/PDF. If you deliberately decline the app, agree on another way to deliver information. Base any QR or name-based check-in on your operations, not on whether someone has the app.

## Getting started

First establish:

- **What should the trip achieve, and for whom?** Recognition, partner relationships, team collaboration, or a combination? Break the goal down for the organizer, for the attendee, and on a partner trip for the invited partner as well, and say how you will recognize success; on an incentive trip that is usually feedback after people return.
- **Who is invited and who has confirmed?** Importing known contacts does not confirm that everyone will travel.
- **What is fixed and what can people choose?** Dates, arrival, departure, room, roommate, transport, activity, and meals.
- **Who approves a change?** Name the organizer and the contacts responsible for transport, accommodation, and on-site operations.
- **When and where will people connect?** Offer an accessible web route; installing the mobile app should not be an obstacle without a purpose.

If all choices are already settled, app access and a clear itinerary may be enough. If not, collect the missing information selectively. Do not create another registration merely because existing information is scattered.

## Details

### Choose the appropriate setup

- **A fixed itinerary for a known group:** Agenda, Venue, Contact, practical information, and change communication.
- **A trip with accommodation or activity choices:** add registration or information collection, Rooming, or reservations where a choice has a real capacity limit.
- **Multiple arrivals and groups:** distinguish dates, transport, and activities so everyone receives the right instructions.
- **A relationship-building or recurring program:** add Networking if it helps people get to know others, arrange meetings, or keep contacts. A group of around 50 can benefit from it too.

### Build a useful foundation

The **attendee app** should quickly answer: what is next, where should I be, what do I need, and whom can I call?

- **Agenda:** meeting and departure times, transfers, activities, and rest. The activity start time is not necessarily the group meeting time.
- **Venue and Custom content:** the hotel, exact pickup point, map, equipment, and participation requirements. Give each transfer an understandable landmark.
- **Contact:** the organizer and operational contacts available to attendees. Internal supplier details do not all belong in visible app content.
- **Access e-mail:** the correct link and a short explanation of why to open the app. An invitation requesting confirmation and an e-mail granting access are not always the same step.

A microsite is useful when people need to understand the trip or confirm participation before entering the app. It is not mandatory for a closed group with everything already arranged.

### Accommodation: count the right unit

Distinguish **people, rooms, beds, and nights**. The attendee count alone is not a hotel booking.

Example: 40 people stay for two nights and eight stay for one. That is **88 person-nights**. If everyone shares twin rooms without other constraints, this means 24 occupied rooms on the first night and 20 on the second: **44 room-nights**. This is a calculation example, not a confirmed reservation or price.

If you use Rooming:

- prepare room types, capacities, and allocation rules;
- verify that contacts needed for roommate selection are assigned to the event;
- handle single-room requests, different stay lengths, and cancellations separately;
- send the hotel a checked list in the agreed format and obtain confirmation of receipt.

Do not assume that an export includes every additional field or that changing the app automatically updates the hotel booking. Someone must own the final allocation, changes, and supplier confirmation.

### Transport and activities: names, not just totals

For every bus or arrival, know **who travels, when, and from where**. Fifty confirmed attendees do not mean fifty people on the same service.

Reservations can help with capacity-limited activities. Allow for travel, instructions, and recovery time between activities; the activity duration alone is not enough. Verify the particular reservation setup before opening it.

To build relationships, give people a simple opportunity to connect: short profiles, a shared task, or a defined time to meet. A feature should support the purpose of the trip, not add obligations.

### Before, during, and after the trip

**Before the trip**

1. Confirm participation, missing choices, and the responsible organizer.
2. Test an ordinary attendee's path: access, their Agenda, the correct hotel or transport, and essential contacts.
3. Before closing supplier information, check both totals and named lists with the suppliers.
4. Prepare change communication and an alternative way to deliver critical instructions. A notification is not proof that everyone read it.

**During the trip**

1. Maintain one current itinerary.
2. Send departure or location changes to the right group and obtain confirmation for critical changes.
3. If you also use a PDF, identify its version and check the destination of its shared link. A previously downloaded PDF does not update itself.

**After the trip**

1. Collect feedback about the purpose of the trip, not only a general rating.
2. Before closing the event, compare actual stays and services used with the confirmed supplier information.
3. Share photos or contacts only with the appropriate audience and within the agreed scope.
4. For recurring groups, use what you learned at the next meeting instead of starting from an empty brief.

## Common pitfalls

### Treating a known contact as a confirmed attendee

Keep invited, confirmed, and canceled participation separate. Replacing a person can affect a room, transport, and an activity—it is not just changing a name.

### A supplier receives a list but does not confirm it

Sending a file is not acceptance of an order. Agree on the format, cutoff, and person who confirms the final version. Happenee does not thereby provide an automatic connection to a hotel's system.

### The itinerary includes only the activities themselves

Allow for transfers, group boarding, and rest. When choices overlap, check whether each person can realistically reach the next location.

### Sensitive information is visible to the whole group

Collect dietary, health, or travel information only as needed and make it available to authorized people. Do not publish it in open app content.
